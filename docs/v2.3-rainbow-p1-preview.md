# Rainbow P1 owner preview

This is the isolated DEBUG review lab approved for P1, not live Rainbow gameplay. The latest owner-approved revision retains richer hues and narrows artwork AND actual hits to a Spark-aimed horizontal lane. Normal startup still opens the existing title. Release builds contain no P1 scene/entry.

## Open it

In Xcode, open Sparkforge and choose **Product → Scheme → Edit Scheme → Run → Arguments**. Add and check `--rainbow-p1` under Arguments Passed On Launch. Use Debug, then Run on your chosen simulator/device when AL's test window is clear. No version/icon, signing, workflow or security changes are needed for the simulator. Remove/uncheck that argument to return to normal startup.

The lab initially selects its fixture Rainbow and starts a full20s timer. Nothing is drafted or granted to a real run, and it writes no progress/save data. Ordinary fixture shot basis10 gives sweep35 before crit/target adjustments. The larger target is a boss-class fixture, not a new boss. The snow-colored target demonstrates existing Whiteout melting on positive damage. Walls are test pieces with an open gap, not an Arena8 map.

## Controls

- Use the existing floating/recentering joystick on the left side to move Spark. Movement stays inside the visible lab; production controls are unchanged.
- **PAUSE** and **LEVEL** toggle separate gameplay holds. Both must be released to resume; backgrounding adds another hold without clearing your pause.
- **LS NEXT** toggles Long Shot T4 for the next launch. During a crossing, its original captured setting/origin remains authoritative for both damage and target tint.
- **PAN** shifts the camera70pt without dragging an existing sweep footprint or resetting its clock.
- **BOARD** replaces fixture targets, retains Rainbow and resets the next launch to20s. **END** cancels the attack and stops ownership. **RESET** clears the entire fixture run; press **SELECT** to choose the fixture Rainbow again. SELECT while already owned cannot reset the clock.

## What to feel and inspect

1. Watch SUPER! count down to the final1s faint aiming band. During that warning, kite Spark vertically: the proposed lane follows his world Y. At launch it locks and stops following him. The rainbow/SUPER! crossing lasts0.75 active seconds; the next20s cycle begins at launch. A battlefield without enemies would still consume the scheduled cycle.
2. The bright **leading vertical line** spans only the captured horizontal lane and deals damage as it meets a body. The lane is one-third of the captured visible battlefield height, centered on Spark’s launch Y. Targets wholly outside it are unharmed; an enemy body partially touching the edge can be hit. The rainbow trail and lettering do not deal later damage. Check that moving doorway targets lose health at contact rather than all at the opening flash.
3. Kite Spark before launch to aim the lane and change wall sightlines. Target tint indicates wall sightline (snow color indicates Whiteout), not a guaranteed lane hit; once the sweep starts, move or toggle LS NEXT and verify that the current sweep keeps its firing snapshot. A clear doorway permits a hit; a wall blocks unless Long Shot was captured on.
4. Pause during warning/crossing and wait, optionally background the app. Resume: the same front continues, victims do not take a second hit and no missed real-time attacks erupt.
5. Try BOARD/END/RESET during a crossing. The old front must disappear without later damage. Re-selecting after RESET waits a full20 active seconds. BOARD retains ownership but also waits20s.
6. Judge whether SUPER! is playful/readable, whether its front/target contact is clear, whether tells/HP labels remain readable and whether the1s cue/0.75s crossing feel right. These are tuning candidates; the20s cadence and approved damage/geometry rules stay fixed.

Brandon owns this interactive feel test. Technical tests/builds do not claim it passed. No phone/iPad FPS, full combat or Switch performance finding is inferred from this small lab.

## Lane clipping boundary

The proposed lane stays centered on Spark. Near the visible battlefield’s top/bottom, intersect it with that captured visible rectangle rather than shifting it away from him. Thus the visible band becomes shorter near an edge (half its proposed height if Spark is exactly at the boundary); it gains no offscreen coverage. Scene controls keep Spark inside the visible field. “Visible battlefield” is viewport intersected with current board bounds, before computing the one-third height. Spark is an aim point, not an outward burst origin. Camera/player motion after firing cannot move the lane. The richer palette remains; narrow lane is now approved, while initial presentation timing/readability still awaits owner feel acceptance.
